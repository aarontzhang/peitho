# Academic Research on Advertising Effectiveness for Static/Display Ads

*Compiled April 2026 | 35+ papers across 8 research domains*

---

## 1. Eye-Tracking Studies on Display Advertisements

**Wedel, M. & Pieters, R. (2000). "Eye Fixations on Advertisements and Memory for Brands: A Model and Findings." Marketing Science, 19(4), 297-312.**
- The number of fixations (not their duration) predicts how much information a consumer extracts from an ad
- Fixations to a brand element directly promote accurate brand memory
- URL: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.19.4.297.11794

**Pieters, R. & Wedel, M. (2004). "Attention Capture and Transfer in Advertising: Brand, Pictorial, and Text-Size Effects." Journal of Marketing, 68(2), 36-50.**
- Tested 1,363 print advertisements with infrared eye-tracking on 3,600+ consumers
- The pictorial element was superior in capturing attention independent of its size
- The text element captured attention in direct proportion to its surface size
- The brand element most effectively transferred attention to other elements
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkg.68.2.36.27794

**Pieters, R., Rosbergen, E. & Wedel, M. (1999). "Visual Attention to Repeated Print Advertising: A Test of Scanpath Theory." Journal of Marketing Research, 36(4), 424-438.**
- Attention duration decreases significantly across advertising repetitions
- Attentional scanpaths (measured through saccades) remain constant across repetitions
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224379903600403

**Higgins, E., Leinenger, M. & Rayner, K. (2014). "Eye Movements When Viewing Advertisements." Frontiers in Psychology, 5, Article 210.**
- Comprehensive review establishing that fixation distributions reveal which ad regions most effectively capture visual attention
- Fixation duration indicates the amount of cognitive and perceptual processing devoted to a region
- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00210/full

**Dreze, X. & Hussherr, F.-X. (2003). "Internet Advertising: Is Anybody Watching?" Journal of Interactive Marketing, 17, 8-23.**
- Used eye-tracking to investigate attention to online advertising
- Found that surfers not only did not fixate banner ads but purposefully avoided looking at them
- Click-through rates are low because of active avoidance; processing occurs at the pre-attentive level
- URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/dir.10063

**Teixeira, T., Wedel, M. & Pieters, R. (2012). "Emotion-Induced Engagement in Internet Video Advertisements." Journal of Marketing Research, 49(2), 144-159.**
- Surprise and joy effectively concentrate attention and retain viewers
- The level of surprise affects attention concentration most, whereas the velocity of joy affects viewer retention most
- The effect of joy is asymmetric: higher gains for increases than losses for decreases
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0207

**Wedel, M. & Pieters, R. (2008). "Eye Tracking for Visual Marketing." Foundations and Trends in Marketing, 1(4), 231-320.**
- Distinguishes between ambient attention (short fixations, long saccades) and focal attention (long fixations, short saccades with deeper processing)
- URL: https://www.nowpublishers.com/article/Details/MKT-011

## 2. Color and Emotion in Advertising

**Labrecque, L.I. & Milne, G.R. (2012). "Exciting Red and Competent Blue: The Importance of Color in Marketing." Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 40, 711-727.**
- Four studies mapping hues onto brand personality dimensions
- Red is linked to excitement, blue to competence and trust, green to sincerity
- Saturation and value amplify brand personality traits
- Color-brand personality congruence strengthens consumer preference and loyalty
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y

**Mehta, R. & Zhu, R. (2009). "Blue or Red? Exploring the Effect of Color on Cognitive Task Performances." Science, 323(5918), 1226-1229.**
- 600+ participants on six cognitive tasks
- Red enhances performance on detail-oriented tasks (memory retrieval, proofreading) by up to 31%
- Blue enhances creative task performance; participants produced twice as many creative outputs under blue
- Red induces avoidance motivation; blue induces approach motivation
- Effects occur outside of conscious awareness
- URL: https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1169144

**Singh, S. (2006). "Impact of Color on Marketing." Management Decision, 44(6), 783-789.**
- People make up their minds within 90 seconds of initial interaction, with 62-90% of assessment based on colors alone
- URL: https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/00251740610673332/full/html

**Cyr, D., Head, M. & Larios, H. (2010). "Colour Appeal in Website Design Within and Across Cultures." International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 68(1), 1-21.**
- Website color appeal is a significant determinant of trust and satisfaction, with differences across cultures
- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581909001116

**Bottomley, P.A. & Doyle, J.R. (2006). "The Interactive Effects of Colors and Products on Perceptions of Brand Logo Appropriateness." Marketing Theory, 6(1), 63-83.**
- Functional products benefit from functional colors; sensory-social products benefit from sensory-social colors
- Color-product congruence enhances brand meaning
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1470593106061263

## 3. Typography and Readability in Advertising

**McCarthy, M.S. & Mothersbaugh, D.L. (2002). "Effects of Typographic Factors in Advertising-Based Persuasion." Psychology & Marketing, 19(7-8), 663-691.**
- Typography significantly influences motivation, opportunity, and ability to process advertising messages
- Effects of various typographic characteristics are highly interactive
- URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mar.10030

**Henderson, P.W., Giese, J.L. & Cote, J.A. (2004). "Impression Management Using Typeface Design." Journal of Marketing, 68(4), 60-72.**
- Studied 210 typefaces with 24 design characteristics, divided into six clusters
- Identified six underlying design dimensions: elaborate, harmony, natural, flourish, weight, and compressed
- Typeface creates strategically valued impressions (pleasing, engaging, reassuring, prominent)
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.68.4.60.42736

**Childers, T.L. & Jass, J. (2002). "All Dressed Up with Something to Say: Effects of Typeface Semantic Associations on Brand Perceptions and Consumer Memory." Journal of Consumer Psychology, 12(2), 93-106.**
- Visual properties of typefaces communicate unique semantic associations distinct from the written words
- Memorability of advertised benefit claims enhanced when typeface semantics, visual cues, and copy claims are consistent
- Serif fonts better for hedonic brands; sans serif for utilitarian brands
- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057740802702271

**Doyle, J.R. & Bottomley, P.A. (2004). "Font Appropriateness and Brand Choice." Journal of Business Research, 57(8), 873-880.**
- Brands were chosen **twice as frequently** when in an appropriate font versus an inappropriate one
- Font appropriateness depends on consistency between connotative meaning of font and product category
- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296302004873

## 4. Visual Complexity in Ads

**Pieters, R., Wedel, M. & Batra, R. (2010). "The Stopping Power of Advertising: Measures and Effects of Visual Complexity." Journal of Marketing, 74(5), 48-60.**
- Distinguishes two types: **feature complexity** (dense perceptual features) vs. **design complexity** (elaborate creative design)
- Analysis of 249 advertisements with eye-tracking
- Feature complexity hurts attention to the brand and attitude toward the ad
- Design complexity helps attention to both the pictorial and the advertisement as a whole
- Design complexity is under direct control of the advertiser
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.74.5.048

**Ning, B., Luo, S., Wang, A. et al. (2023). "Effects of Banner Ad Type, Web Content Type and Theme Consistency on Banner Blindness." Cognitive Processing.**
- 67 participants browsed pages with different combinations of text/picture content and banner ads
- Theme consistency between ad and web content moderates banner blindness effects
- Text-type banner ads in picture-type web content with consistent themes reduce banner blindness most effectively
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10339-023-01131-7

**Li, Y. & Xie, Y. (2020). "Is a Picture Worth a Thousand Words?" Journal of Marketing Research, 57(1), 1-19.**
- Including images increases retweets by 119-213% and likes by 87-151%
- High-quality, professionally shot pictures lead to higher engagement
- Human faces increase engagement by 38-291% on Twitter but not on Instagram
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022243719881113

## 5. Persuasion and Advertising Copy

**Rosengren, S., Eisend, M., Koslow, S. & Dahlen, M. (2020). "A Meta-Analysis of When and How Advertising Creativity Works." Journal of Marketing, 84(6), 39-56.**
- Meta-analysis covering 93 data sets from 67 papers yielding 878 effect sizes
- Both originality and appropriateness matter
- Creativity effects are stronger for high-involvement products
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022242920929288

**Reinartz, W. & Saffert, P. (2013). "Creativity in Advertising: When It Works and When It Doesn't." Harvard Business Review, 91(June), 106-112.**
- 437 TV ad campaigns for 90 FMCG brands linked to sales data
- Elaboration had a far more powerful sales effect than originality
- A euro invested in a highly creative campaign had nearly double the sales impact of a noncreative campaign
- URL: https://hbr.org/2013/06/creativity-in-advertising-when-it-works-and-when-it-doesnt

**Gkiouzepas, L. & Hogg, M.K. (2011). "Articulating a New Framework for Visual Metaphors in Advertising." Journal of Advertising, 40(1), 103-120.**
- Synthesized visuals with conceptually similar metaphorical objects provoke greater elaboration
- Synthesized metaphors elicit more favorable attitudes toward the ad and brand
- URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2753/JOA0091-3367400107

**Luan, L. et al. (2025). "The Influence of Affective and Cognitive Appeals on Persuasion Outcomes." Journal of Communication, 75(2), 101.**
- Meta-analysis of 133 samples across 22 countries (N = 29,338)
- Affective appeals are relatively more effective in collectivistic societies
- Both appeals are similarly effective in individualistic societies
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/joc/article/75/2/101/7916611

**Mehta, A. & Purvis, S.C. (2006). "Reconsidering Recall and Emotion in Advertising." Journal of Advertising Research, 46(1), 49-56.**
- Emotional advertising is not penalized by recall measures as commonly believed
- Emotional content in well-executed commercials can actually boost recall
- URL: https://www.journalofadvertisingresearch.com/content/46/1/49

**Edell, J.A. & Burke, M.C. (1987). "The Power of Feelings in Understanding Advertising Effects." Journal of Consumer Research, 14(3), 421-433.**
- Feelings occur as a result of viewing ads and are distinct from cognitive judgments
- Both feelings and ad-characteristic judgments contribute to advertising outcomes
- URL: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/24098526_The_Power_of_Feelings_in_Understanding_Advertising_Effects

## 6. Banner Blindness and Attention

**Benway, J.P. & Lane, D.M. (1998). "Banner Blindness: Web Searchers Often Miss 'Obvious' Links." Internetworking: ITG Newsletter, 1(3).**
- Coined the term "banner blindness"
- Experimental banners were found only 58% of the time vs. 94% for control items
- URL: https://www.ruf.rice.edu/~lane/papers/banner_blindness.pdf

**Sajjacholapunt, P. & Ball, L.J. (2014). "The Influence of Banner Advertisements on Attention and Memory: Human Faces with Averted Gaze Can Enhance Advertising Effectiveness." Frontiers in Psychology, 5, Article 166.**
- Faces with averted gaze increased attention to the banner overall and to advertising text and product
- Mutual gaze directed attention to the face itself rather than ad content
- Averted gaze enhanced both explicit memory (brand recognition) and implicit memory
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3941030/

**Sapronov, F. & Gorbunova, E. (2022). "The Role of Emotional Information in Banner Blindness." Frontiers in Psychology.**
- Counterintuitive finding: neutral-valence banners were recognized better than positive or negative ones
- Emotionally charged content is categorized as typical advertising and actively avoided
- Attempting to make ads more emotionally arousing may paradoxically increase banner blindness
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9128483/

**Lee, J. & Ahn, J.-H. (2012). "Attention to Banner Ads and Their Effectiveness." International Journal of Electronic Commerce, 17(1), 119-137.**
- Animation in banner ads attracts less attention than static ads and reduces the positive effect of attention on memory
- Even when users don't consciously notice a banner ad, attitudes toward the brand are influenced (implicit/preattentive processing)
- URL: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.2753/JEC1086-4415170105

**Janiszewski, C. (1993). "Preattentive Mere Exposure Effects." Journal of Consumer Research, 20(3), 376-392.**
- Mere exposure to a brand name encourages more favorable attitudes even without conscious recollection
- Implications for understanding how banner ads may still influence consumers even when "not seen"
- URL: https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/20/3/376/1838974

## 7. Social Media Ad Effectiveness

**Lee, D., Hosanagar, K. & Nair, H.S. (2018). "Advertising Content and Consumer Engagement on Social Media: Evidence from Facebook." Management Science, 64(11), 5105-5131.**
- Content-coded 106,316 Facebook messages across 782 companies
- Humor and emotion associated with higher engagement (likes, comments, shares)
- Directly informative content associated with lower engagement in isolation, but higher engagement when combined with brand personality attributes
- Deals and promotions drive click-throughs (conversion path)
- URL: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2902

**Kusumasondjaja, S. (2019). "Exploring the Role of Visual Aesthetics and Presentation Modality in Luxury Fashion Brand Communication on Instagram." Journal of Fashion Marketing and Management.**
- Expressive aesthetic images received more likes and comments than classical aesthetics
- Significant interaction: expressive aesthetics + audio-visual modality generated the most responses
- URL: https://www.emerald.com/jfmm/article-abstract/24/1/15/212631

**Li, Y. & Xie, Y. (2020). "Is a Picture Worth a Thousand Words?" Journal of Marketing Research, 57(1), 1-19.**
- Images boost engagement: retweets up 119-213%, likes up 87-151%
- Human faces in images increase Twitter engagement by 38-291% but not Instagram
- Image-text fit induces higher engagement on Twitter but not Instagram
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022243719881113

## 8. Neuromarketing and Ad Design

**Vecchiato, G., Astolfi, L., De Vico Fallani, F., Toppi, J. et al. (2011). "On the Use of EEG or MEG Brain Imaging Tools in Neuromarketing Research." Computational Intelligence and Neuroscience, Article 643489.**
- Increased prefrontal and parietal activity during memory encoding correlates with later ad recall
- Left prefrontal cortex activates during pleasant ad viewing; right-sided activation during disliked content
- Cultural differences in neural engagement: Chinese audiences showed greater engagement during group scenes; Italian subjects during individual performers
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3180786/

**Ohme, R., Reykowska, D., Wiener, D. & Choromanska, A. (2009). "Analysis of Neurophysiological Reactions to Advertising Stimuli by Means of EEG and Galvanic Skin Response Measures." Journal of Neuroscience, Psychology, and Economics, 2, 21-31.**
- Two nearly identical ad versions for a skincare product generated significantly different neurophysiological impact
- EEG and GSR can detect differences in ad effectiveness not captured by self-report measures
- URL: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242466880

**Ohme, R., Reykowska, D., Wiener, D. & Choromanska, A. (2010). "Application of Frontal EEG Asymmetry to Advertising Research." Journal of Economic Psychology, 31(5), 785-793.**
- Frontal EEG asymmetry is a reliable indicator of approach/withdrawal motivation toward ads
- Left frontal activation indicates positive engagement; right frontal indicates avoidance
- URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167487010000310

**Bolls, P.D., Lang, A. & Potter, R.F. (2001). "The Effects of Message Valence and Listener Arousal on Attention, Memory, and Facial Muscular Responses to Radio Advertisements." Communication Research, 28(5), 627-651.**
- Validated facial EMG for measuring emotional valence in response to ads
- Negative messages receive more attention (measured by heart rate deceleration) than positive ones
- URL: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/009365001028005003

## Summary of Key Design Principles Supported by Research

1. **Visual simplicity wins** -- Feature complexity hurts attention and attitudes; design complexity (creative, elaborated) helps (Pieters, Wedel & Batra 2010)
2. **Faces with averted gaze** direct attention to ad content and boost memory (Sajjacholapunt & Ball 2014)
3. **Color-brand congruence** is critical -- red for excitement, blue for trust/competence (Labrecque & Milne 2012; Mehta & Zhu 2009)
4. **Font-product congruence** doubles brand choice rates (Doyle & Bottomley 2004)
5. **Animation can backfire** -- static ads can outperform animated ones for attention and memory (Lee & Ahn 2012)
6. **Emotional content boosts recall** when well-executed, and humor/emotion drive social engagement (Mehta & Purvis 2006; Lee, Hosanagar & Nair 2018)
7. **Creativity matters, but elaboration beats originality** for sales impact (Reinartz & Saffert 2013)
8. **Preattentive processing** means even "ignored" ads can influence brand attitudes (Janiszewski 1993)
9. **Images dramatically outperform text** for social media engagement: 87-213% increases (Li & Xie 2020)
10. **Neural measures (EEG)** detect ad effectiveness differences invisible to self-report (Ohme et al. 2009)
